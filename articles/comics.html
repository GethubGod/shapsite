§ I — A Kids' Genre

Comic books prior to the 90s focused primarily on kid-friendly topics that followed straightforward plotlines of good and evil, with occasional references to deeper themes. Of course, this made it extremely popular among younger audiences, and to the older demographic, it was seen as disposable, low-quality media. All of this changed in 1986 with the release of The Dark Knight Returns and Watchmen. These two comics completely revolutionized the comic industry by captivating the adult audience.

Two books, one revolution · 1986
1986
Frank Miller · DC

The Dark Knight Returns

Frank Miller's Dark Knight Returns replaced the child centric tones with a mature story leaning into the realities of being a superhero, telling the story of a Batman returning to save the dystopian, crime-ridden Gotham. Continuing the realism, the comic explored real world issues of cold war nuclear fears, sensationalized media with 24 hour news, and the growing distrust in the government.

Watchmen

The Watchmen chose to deconstruct superheroes, emphasizing their human flaws and posing the moral dilemma of choosing the cost of choosing the greater good. It explored similar issues of mutually assured nuclear destruction, corruption in the government, and the question of whether vigilante actions were effective.

§ II — Page Becomes Screen

Comic book adaptations have always coexisted with their source materials, but it wasn't until the early 2000s that they became mainstream. In the 80s and 90s, comic book adaptations consisted of child-centric movies such as Tim Burton's Batman and Superman, which focused on a goofy and hopeful tone that had a campy feel similar to the comic books of the same time period. Like their comic book counterparts, they were niche and were mainly appealing to the younger demographic.

As time went on, technological improvements brought us modern CGI technology, allowing directors to tell more complex and bigger stories. This brought a new wave of realistic superhero movies, first with Spiderman and X-Men that chose to incorporate value emotional complexity over the campy feel. Following the events of 9/11, a cultural shift emerged that desired more realistic and emotionally complex cinema. Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight Trilogy was the first to do it successfully. The film had broke all time records, becoming the 4th highest grossing film of all time.

By choosing to remove the cartoonish elements, Nolan placed Batman in a gritty world with real world dilemmas which mirrored the revolution that The Dark Knight Returns and Watchmen had brought to comic books. Now superhero movies have become a multi billion dollar industry that dominates film and cultures around us. We can see this through the Marvel Cinematic Universe, known for movies such as Iron Man and Avengers Endgame, consisting of interconnected movies and tv shows spanning 18 years and made over $31 billion at the global box office.

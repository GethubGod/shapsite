§ I — Roots

Since the 1990s, rap music has transformed itself dramatically in order to establish itself as one of the most dominant music genres of our modern day. Rap music of the 1980s and 1990s primarily focused on elements and public criticism. It was symbolized by popular figures such as Tupac Shakur, Eminem, and several others. The rap music executed during this time period included an incorporation of sounds that revealed community struggles and provided a voice to those typically ignored by the media. The efforts would eventually lay the basic foundation for rap music of today's modern times.

Two of the music genres that were primarily focused on during the 90s were gangster rap and grunge music which were both elements that featured themes of rebellion and raw emotions. Grunge music primarily focused on rock acts such as Nirvana and Pearl Jam who focused on the frustrations that were apparent within those times. In contrast, gangster rap brought perspectives of the frustrations by existing oppression within society. Each of these musical genres would eventually help shape and define the culture of the decade which eventually carries its influence into modern day rap.

§ II — The Pivot

Today rap music creates newer and more accessible forms of music. For instance, artists like Kendrick Lamar and Drake have given the genre a newer more diverse perspective that an individual can view rap with. It's currently one of the most dominant music of modern day and it's also intersecting with today's fashion and the fashion industry. The music has developed into a subsection of several new genres, however. Once a niche movement in music, now a universal form of music, one that defines modern pop culture. Therefore, rap remains one of the most influential music genres of today's age and a defining characteristic of the lives of a generation. So, as shown, both of these songs include their own elements of confidence, however, in different ways. In U Can't Touch This by MC Hammer, the tone seems very upbeat and fun. MC Hammer proves to himself and the listeners that he's confident in both his talents and his successes. However, In Rather Lie by Playboi Carti and The Weeknd, the tone is more emotional and apparently conflicted. While there are apparent struggles within the relationships expressed in the song, there is also a sense of confidence from each individual in themselves when expressing themselves despite the struggles that they have been through in their pasts. Hammer takes a more playful and upbeat tone in the lyrics, while Carti and the Weeknd take on a more thoughtful and melodic tone.
